What is broken today?

Rooms don’t behave the way the timetable says they do.

Free periods, cancelled lectures, extra practicals and irregular lab bookings shift the campus daily — and nobody can see it.

01

No live visibility

Faculty and admin cannot verify whether a scheduled room is actually in use — “is this room free?” stays a guess.

02

Timetable ≠ reality

Cancellations, extra lectures and unbooked lab sessions never reach the official schedule.

03

Energy runs on habit

Lights, fans and projectors stay on in empty rooms until a person happens to notice and switch them off.

04

No utilisation record

Renovation, timetable and new-section decisions are taken without any evidence of how rooms are really used.

What exactly are we building?

Five layers. One platform.

The sensor and relay are the mandatory foundation. The innovation is the decision layer built around them.

1

Smart Room Sensing

  • PIR / ultrasonic sensor + ESP32 per room
  • Real-time occupied / vacant state
  • Occupancy count as stretch feature
2

Intelligent Occupancy Engine

  • Live sensor data + official timetable
  • Bookings, time of day, history
  • Classifies six distinct room states
3

Automated Energy Control

  • Vacancy past threshold → relay OFF
  • Occupancy resumes → circuit restored
  • Every switching event timestamped
4

Centralised Dashboard

  • Live campus status and heatmap
  • Alerts, overrides, room lookup
  • Role-based faculty / admin views
5

Analytics & Intelligence

  • Utilisation and mismatch analytics
  • Peak-usage and anomaly detection
  • Energy-saving estimation per room

Where is the intelligence?

We separate automation from intelligence — honestly.

IoT Layer (Phase 1)

Deterministic, rule-based

  • PIR / ultrasonic sensing
  • ESP32 state machine
  • Relay switching
  • Event logging

Intelligence Layer (Phase 2+)

Analytics on collected history

  • Timetable mismatch detection
  • Historical utilisation analysis
  • Peak-usage prediction
  • Anomaly & odd-hour detection

We do not claim PIR detection is AI. Rule-based automation ships first; predictive models are trained only once real occupancy history exists.

Why is this different?

Five things a motion-sensor project cannot do

01

Context-Aware Occupancy

Sensor data is read together with the official timetable and booking context — so the system knows the difference between an empty room at 11 AM on a Tuesday and an empty room at 9 PM.

02

Closed-Loop Automation

Detect → decide → physically control → verify → log. The loop closes on real hardware and confirms that the circuit actually changed state.

03

Timetable Reality Check

Identifies “booked but empty” and “occupied but unbooked” rooms automatically and routes each to the right person for review.

04

Campus Utilisation Intelligence

Historical occupancy becomes evidence for room allocation, timetable revision, new sections and infrastructure planning.

05

Explainable Energy Automation

Every automated switching action carries a timestamp, a reason and a room-level record — auditable, not a black box.
